Build the setlist

  • Song pool. Add a song once — working name, print title, key, BPM, length — and reuse it on every setlist.
  • Templates and copies. Start from scratch, from a template, or from an independent copy of any night’s set.
  • Sections, breaks and encores. Dividers that never eat a song number. Encores keep counting correctly.
  • Notes on every song. “Hold the last chorus.” On the page when you want it, off when you don’t.
  • Custom logos. Round artist logos, headers with name, logo or both, and a free-text line under the title for version notes.
  • Print that behaves. A4 and US Letter. Columns stay uniform on every row; long sets flip to landscape before text gets unreadably small.
  • Export presets. Choose your columns once — capo, tuning, pack, stand, gauge — save the preset, use it all tour.
  • Your footer, your call. Your name and business name print in the footer only when you switch them on.
  • Offline first. Everything works with no signal. Work syncs through your own iCloud — never our servers, because we don’t run any.

For guitar techs

This is the deep end. Setlist Designer speaks guitar world.

  • A real guitar pool. Make, model, nickname, finish with a colour swatch, serial number, bridge type, string gauge, photos from camera or files. Photos never print.
  • Per-song assignment. Assign a guitar to each musician for each song. Sits-out prints an empty cell — never a placeholder.
  • Tunings, spelled out. Default tuning per guitar, one-off overrides per song, full string-by-string spellings when E♭ isn’t enough.
  • Capos where they belong. Capo lives on the song assignment, not the guitar — because that’s how it works.
  • Wireless packs and wired. Pack numbers per guitar, overridable per song. Wired shows as wired, not as a missing number.
  • Stands and racks. A slot per guitar so it lives in the same place every night — overridable per setlist and per song. Walk to the right stand every time.
  • Double-booking warnings. Same guitar on two musicians? Same pack number in one song? You get a clear warning — in the editor and again in Show Mode. You can keep it, but you’ll know.
  • The guitar sheet. A printable change table — song by song, musician by musician, with pack icons, tunings, capos and stand slots. Tape it to the workbox.

Show Mode

White on black, readable under stage lights, and it never sleeps mid-set.

  • Now and next. The current song’s full rig on top. The next song, huge, underneath — with every change between them.
  • Changes as a checklist. “From Acoustic HH. Tuning E♭ → Open D. Pack 1 → Pack 6. Stand 2 → 1.” One line per action, per musician.
  • Bring-ons and collections. Knows when a musician joins, sits out, or hands a guitar back — and says so.
  • Safety warnings. Unassigned musician on the next song, or two musicians on one pack — flagged before the cue, not after.
  • Built for adrenaline. Big targets, deliberate swipes, no accidental song changes. Arrow keys and shortcuts on Mac and iPad.
  • Remembers its place. Leave and come back — each setlist reopens on the song you were on.
